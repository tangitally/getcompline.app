How it works

Compline knows which parking spots are worth watching, and turns Sentry Mode on and off for you.

How does Compline decide?

Every time you park, Compline scores that spot from 0 to 100 using local crime reports, parked-car crashes, how busy and well lit the area is, and the time of day.

  • Local crime reports
  • Parked-car crashes
  • How busy the area is
  • Lighting
  • Time of day

38

  • Quiet
  • Watchful
  • Elevated
  • Hot

You'll see that score colored Quiet, Watchful, Elevated, or Hot, and it's an estimate, not a promise your car is safe.

The ring above is our demo: a score of 38, shown as medium risk, on a sample spot.

Where does it work?

You can use Compline in Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach.

If we don't have much local data for a spot, Compline suggests leaving Sentry on.

Can I set my own schedule?

Compline turns Sentry on where your car needs watching and off where it doesn't, or you can set your own on and off times at Home.

  1. Sentry turns on at a riskier spot.

  2. It stays off at a calmer one.

  3. You can set on and off times at Home.

More saved places are coming later, and this stays off until you turn it on.

You sign in with Tesla, then approve Compline in the Tesla app, and you can delete your account anytime.

What will I hear from it?

We'll text you when you park.

No calls, no spam.

You'll get the spot's score and whether Sentry is on, not the address.

How much can it save?

Left on around the clock, Sentry can use about 216 kWh a month. Compline keeps more of that for driving.

That 216 is 7.2 kWh a day times 30 days, and 7.2 kWh is 24 hours at 300 watts, from owner reports Electrek cited on 22 Feb 2024. Tesla doesn't publish a watt figure, and that same note said a cut was in the works, so today's use may be lower.

You're parked 14 hours. In this example, 4 of those hours are risky enough for Sentry, so it stays off for 10 hours.

That's an estimated 90 kWh a month and 1,095 kWh a year, or about 360 miles a month and 4,380 miles a year.

The dollar range assumes Supercharger prices of $0.57 to $0.63 per kWh, so this is an estimated $51.30-$56.70 a month and $624.15-$689.85 a year. What you save depends on how often Compline switches Sentry off for you.

Putting that energy back at Tesla's published 250 kW peak would take an estimated 21.6 minutes a month. The app shows your car's real numbers.

216 kWh/month = 7.2 kWh/day × 30. 7.2 kWh/day = 24 hours × 300 W ÷ 1,000.

3.00 kWh/day = 10 hours × 300 W ÷ 1,000

90 kWh/month = 3.00 × 30. 1,095 kWh/year = 3.00 × 365

$1.71-$1.89/day from 3.00 × $0.57 = $1.71 and 3.00 × $0.63 = $1.89. $51.30-$56.70/month from 90 × $0.57 = $51.30 and 90 × $0.63 = $56.70. $624.15-$689.85/year from 1,095 × $0.57 = $624.15 and 1,095 × $0.63 = $689.85

12 miles/day = 3.00 ÷ 0.25. 360 miles/month = 90 ÷ 0.25. 4,380 miles/year = 1,095 ÷ 0.25

0.72 minutes/day = 3.00 ÷ 250 × 60. 21.6 minutes/month = 90 ÷ 250 × 60. 262.8 minutes/year = 1,095 ÷ 250 × 60

If you charge at home instead, the U.S. residential average was 18.16¢/kWh in August 2026.

  • Electrek, 22 Feb 2024, for the 7.2 kWh a day owner reports and Drew Baglino's note that the team was working on about a 40% cut. The Model S owner's manual says power use may increase when Sentry is on, and doesn't give watts.
  • Electrek, 13 Oct 2026, citing Paren's Q3 2026 report: a 56.9¢ non-member Supercharger average, and 63.7¢ at peak where the price changes by time of day. This example rounds that pair to $0.57-$0.63/kWh. The same report's member average was 39.6¢/kWh.
  • EIA Table 5.3, August 2026 residential average of 18.16¢/kWh, for the home-charging line only.
  • fueleconomy.gov, 25 kWh/100 mi for the 2026 Tesla Model Y Long Range RWD, which is where the miles come from.
  • Tesla Support says V3 Superchargers peak at up to 250 kW, and that a real session is often slower. Tesla doesn't publish one national price.
